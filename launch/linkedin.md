Give your AI JEV wings.

I've been putting TypeSafe's JEV to work: first in my morning agent's triage and classification, then in a paired test across 429 documents.

Now I've built JEVRIEL, a skill and plugin designed to be the easiest portal for builders, developers and curious AI users to start JEVing their work.

Bring a new project. Bring an existing app that runs entirely on LLM calls. Bring the daily workflow you suspect could move faster. JEVRIEL teaches your AI agent to inspect it, find a useful decision point, integrate JEV and measure what changed.

Daniel Kahneman's Thinking, Fast and Slow is, in my opinion, one of the most important books ever written. His System 1 and System 2 gave me a lasting way to think about judgment. They also remind me that fast judgment can be wrong.

JEV gives software a fast decision capability: choose a route, classify an input, score a known option or assess whether evidence supports a claim. Independent questions can run together, returning typed judgments that code can use directly.

I see this as a breakthrough in how we build with AI. A decision capability we can place throughout the workflow, helping the larger agent spend its reasoning where it matters. An AI catalyst.

In our 429-document experiment, JEV's median request was about five times faster than hosted GPT-5.6 Luna. It also produced more invalid batches: 12 out of 489, versus 2 for GPT. Human accuracy scoring is pending. The case study includes those limitations.

That's why JEVRIEL comes with a Flight Test: a short report on latency, cost, quality and reliability, with the evidence behind it. You also get suggestions for where to use JEV next.

One-line installation for Codex or Claude Code, with your choice of JEV API provider. Four use-case guides. A public benchmark you can run. And an invitation to contribute your own Flight Test, including the ones where JEV doesn't help.

Start JEVing your work:
https://github.com/thehan-co/jevriel
